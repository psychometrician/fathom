## Why this one, and why it is deliberately unexciting

**The rule this choice has to obey**: a document picked to break the newest
constant is guaranteed to find something and therefore cannot measure
convergence. Files 17, 18 and 19 closed the gate precisely because they were
ordinary. `61af6458…` has described **no unseen document at all** and the gate
counter is at **0**, so this file's only job is to say whether the design has
stopped moving.

Chosen because **scholarly metadata is a kind of document the corpus does not
have**, and because bibliographic records are ordinary in the specific way that
matters here: written by many publishers to one schema, over decades, with
every optional field genuinely optional. Nothing about it was chosen to test
`VOCAB_GROWTH`, the fold, or anything else repaired today.

> **It was NOT chosen to fill a gap in `corpus/README.md`'s "Wanted" list, and
> that is deliberate.** A gap-filling choice is an adversarial choice wearing a
> different hat — the polymorphism entry there records four files picked to
> exhibit a property, three of which failed to. **An ordinary file is one picked
> without regard to what the probe is good or bad at**, and the price is that it
> may find nothing. Finding nothing is the result this run is for.

**One departure, stated because it weakens the specimen.** `corpus/README.md`
prefers documents no API has tidied, and this is an API's own envelope around
data it did not author. The records inside are publisher deposits and are as
ragged as the real thing; the `status` / `message-type` / `message` wrapper is
Crossref's. **If the probe spends its description on the three-key envelope
instead of the 1,000 records, that is a finding and not an accident of the
choice.**

## What this file disconfirmed

**One defect, and it is not in anything repaired today.** Recorded, not repaired
— rule 5.

**Defect 24 — a split that is right for 896 of 1,000 records is vetoed by the
104 it is wrong for.** The document is twelve kinds and says so in a field named
`type`. Measured on the frozen build:

```
folded emptiness 0.4454        the halving rule wants worst < 0.2227
  book-chapter       822   0.2166   <- PASSES, and it is 82% of the document
  journal-article    104   0.2629   <- FAILS, by 0.04
  dataset             44   0.0000
  book                16   0.1174
  component            5   0.0000
  journal-issue/journal/proceedings-article/book-section/
  book-part/monograph/posted-content   1-2 each   0.0000
```

**Eight of twelve groups reach exactly zero and the dominant group passes.**
The weighted result is **0.2073**, which clears the threshold comfortably.
`discriminator()` returns `None` because the rule is a **maximum over groups**,
so the single group holding 10.4% of the records decides for all of them.

> **This is the mirror image of the two false positives the worst-group rule was
> introduced to prevent.** On `05-fhir-bundle`, `identifier[].system` went 44% →
> 31% and `item[].sequence` 49% → 46% — splits where *nothing* improved, and the
> max caught both. Here almost everything improves and one small group does not.
> **The rule has now been shown to fail in both directions, and the corpus has one
> example of each.**

`type` has **12 distinct values over 1,000 records**, well inside `KIND_MAX = 24`,
and disorder is 0.4454, well over the 0.2 floor. **Neither of the two constants
item 14 wants an adversary for is what refused this split.**

### What it confirmed

- **`folds()` and the saturation verdict generalise** — prediction 7, above.
- **`fathom probe` is byte-identical on its second unseen document**, and the

- **List-columns again**: `rows()` on `message.items` returns 58 columns of which
  `title`, `container-title`, `ISSN`, `isbn-type`, `license`, `link`, `author`

- **The one type change is a data-quality artifact, not a schema one**:
  `issued.date-parts` is `array[2] number` on 998 records and `array[2] null` on
  two — a date deposited as `[[null]]`.

### 1. THE SPLIT: three tools reach the probe's own numbers, and one of them is the probe's

Entry 17's fifteen-line jq search, **unchanged except for the path to the

rule wants a worst group under 0.2227; `type` misses by 0.04 and its weighted
figure passes comfortably. **jqr and DuckDB compute the identical numbers.**

> **THE PROBE'S RANKING IS NOT WRONG — ONLY ITS GATE IS.** Defect 24 has been
> recorded as "a split that is right for 896 of 1,000 is vetoed by the 104 it is
> wrong for". This is the first measurement to show that the probe *picks the
> right field* and then refuses it, which is a narrower and more repairable
> statement. Entry 17 showed the same search agreeing with the probe where the
> probe was RIGHT; this is the other half.

### 2. A frame cannot represent the benefit of a split, and it is arithmetic

pandas and polars independently price the same split on the same field:

| | worst | weighted | unsplit |
|---|---|---|---|
| jq / jqr / DuckDB (`json_keys`) | 0.2629 | **0.2073** | 0.4454 |
| pandas (`groupby`) | 0.634 | **0.443** | 0.443 |
| polars (`group_by`) | 0.667 | **0.445** | 0.445 |

**The weighted figure equals the unsplit figure exactly, in both frames, and
necessarily so.** A frame has committed to all 71 columns, so grouping moves no
holes: the total is fixed and the size-weighted mean of the group emptinesses is
the global emptiness, *for every possible split*.

> **The entire benefit of splitting is that each group needs fewer columns**,
> and that is precisely what a frame gives up when it is built. So pandas and
> polars do not merely *lack a verb* for the fourth operation — the rectangle
> cannot represent the quantity the search is searching for. **DuckDB can
> compute it both ways, and only by leaving the relational world for
> `json_keys`.** It contains both answers and nothing in it prefers one.

### 3. The `$` partial-matching trap FIRES, for the first time in seven documents

Entries 13, 14, 15, 18, 20 and 25 all recorded prefix pairs and **zero
exposure**, and concluded *more pairs is not more danger*. Every one of those
documents had the short key present on every record. **This one has 40 of 57
fields sometimes absent.**

| short key | longer matches | absent on | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `issue` | `issued` | **905** | **FIRES** |
| `published` | `published-online`, `-other`, `-print` | 2 | ambiguous, safe |
| `publisher` | `publisher-location` | 0 | n/a |
| `reference` | `reference-count`, `references-count` | 465 | ambiguous, safe |

**On 905 of 1,000 works `w$issue` silently returns `issued` — the publication
date standing in for an issue number**, with no warning. `w[["issue"]]` is NULL,
correctly.

> **AND AMBIGUITY PROTECTS.** R partial-matches only when the longer match is
> UNIQUE, so `reference` — absent on 465 works and a prefix of *two* longer
> names — returns NULL and is safe. **More pairs on one short key makes it
> safer.** The rule the first six documents produced was right for the wrong
> reason: the condition is an **absent short key plus exactly one longer match**,
> and no earlier entry could see it because no earlier entry had an absent short
> key at all.

### 5. Hyphens: 20 of 57 field names, and five different escaping rules

| | |
|---|---|
| polars, tidyjson, rrapply, glom, pydash | **pay nothing** — a name is a string argument |
| jmespath | **LexerError**, refuses to parse `message-version` |

### 6. The one type change, and a tension in the probe's own rules

The probe reports exactly one site: `issued.date-parts`, **`array[2] number` on
998 records and `array[2] null` on 2**.

- **pandas, polars, jsonlite: invisible.** polars types it `List(List(Int64))`
  and the nulls become null Int64s.
- **jmespath: cannot see it even told the path** — `[]` drops nulls, so its
  census reports 998 numbers and misses the two that matter.
- **jq, DuckDB, purrr, glom, pydash: reachable only by indexing `[0][0]`**,
  which is knowing the answer before asking.
- **ijson sees it**, because a `null` event and a `number` event differ at that
  prefix — and *only* in a census that counts null as a kind.

> **FLAGGED FOR THE AUTHOR, NOT SETTLED HERE.** Defect 11 and `design/axes.py`
> both say **a null is not a type**, and this document's only reported type
> change is a difference that is *entirely a null*. `varies()` discards a bare
> `array` beside `array[...]` and keeps `array[2] null` beside `array[2] number`,
> so the two rules point opposite ways on this one site.

## The fourteenth tool, 2026-08-11 — `try-tidyr.R`

**Rectangling CURES the `$` trap, and by the very behaviour that destroys the
absent/null distinction.**

On the parsed list, `rec$issue` silently returns `issued` — a publication date
standing in for an issue number — on **905 of 1,000** works, because `issue` is
absent there and `issued` is the single longer match. **After `unnest_wider` it
cannot happen**: both `issue` and `issued` exist as columns on every row, so `$`
finds an exact match and the 905 come back NA. 95 present, 905 NA, nothing
borrowed.

> **One property, two consequences, opposite signs.** Every record getting every
> column is exactly why the short name is always present — and exactly why
> **ABSENT and NULL arrive as the same NA**, the distinction this entry and
> entry 23 both turn on. *It removes a silent wrong answer by removing the
> information that made it possible.*

`unnest_auto` is right (*"17 names in common"*, 1,000 x 57, 34 list-columns).
`reference` gives **18,155 rows**, matching the probe's own figure for
`$.message.items[].reference[]`, and drops 465 works; `ISSN` and `issn-type`
each drop 565, `subtitle` 963.

**Question 5 is CANNOT here**, and pointedly: this entry's only reported type
change is `issued.date-parts`, `array[2] number` on 998 works and `array[2]
null` on 2 — **a list-column cannot show that at all.**

**Pricing the split on `type` is measured to the last digit on a smaller
document** — see `../../19-chicago-salaries/NOTES.md`, where the same frame
arithmetic loses a perfect split entirely.
